# Beginner lesson language review — v17.18.12

Aim: explain each idea in language an elementary or middle-school reader could follow, without talking down to adults. This is an editorial review, not a measured reading-grade certification.

## Changes

- Define laying back as choosing a shorter shot to avoid trouble, then explain that the next shot is longer.
- Explain dispersion as the spread of shots. Define the short side and the roomier side of the green with direct examples.
- Lead penalty explanations with what happens to the player's score or next shot.
- Replace guided-result “water exposure” wording with “shots in water,” using the same calculated percentages.
- Shorten the older course-management tutorial within Lessons. The newer Home/Menu Introduction is unchanged.

Examples: “pro-player reference” becomes “professional golf data we use”; “illustrative spreads and aims” becomes “example patterns and targets”; explanation cards now say “Look at the picture, then choose Next.”

## Accuracy and preservation

OB still means count the shot, add one penalty stroke, and replay from the previous spot. Water relief explanations retain the dependence on the penalty-area markings and crossing point. Bunkers and rough do not receive automatic penalty strokes.

Historical professional reference values remain unchanged. The 50–100 and 100–150 yard differences are almost equal; the wording does not claim otherwise. Values are averages rather than promises about a player's score.

No fixtures, seeds, scoring, shot physics, lesson completion versions or storage formats changed.
