Would a different carry pattern change the best target?

Experimental research page. The main game's scoring is unchanged. The two new patterns use illustrative assumptions, not measured handicap averages. Nothing you do here saves progress.

Each comparison holds the club, proportional swing scaling, lateral misses, weather and 4,000 underlying random samples fixed. Only depth errors change. A different target changes swing fraction using the same club. Approaches have no rollout here.

Comparison numbers and experimental assumptions

The grid varies aim ±18 yd sideways and ±24 yd in depth around green center (6 × 8 yd spacing), plus the named targets. It includes only targets using 75–100% of the held club's stock carry. “Best” means best in this limited set, not a global optimum. These scope limits are experimental. A different club or wider search may change the answer.

The shifted pattern subtracts 0.5244 original depth standard deviations, placing stated carry at approximately P70. Width stays unchanged. The mixture scales ordinary depth errors to 65% of current spread, then gives 20% of draws an additional short loss of 1.5 original depth standard deviations. Lateral errors are unchanged. These values are examples to inspect, not values selected from a golfer dataset.

All costs retain the existing Tour reference and current hazard rules. Displayed decision scores compare with this pattern's own sampled best; they are experimental and cannot measure improvement across different patterns. “Short” refers to carry relative to intended carry, not short of the green. Independent sample costs check sensitivity at the displayed target; they do not independently repeat the target search.
